## Why dandruff gets worse in winter

Men's Health spoke with several dermatologists this season, and their explanation goes beyond "the cold dries you out". Low temperatures and heated indoor air pull moisture from the scalp, but they also disturb its natural balance and weaken the skin barrier. The scalp becomes more sensitive, more irritated and more prone to flaking.

The twist: a stressed scalp may produce extra oil to compensate. That can feed dandruff or make existing flakes stand out more, which then brings back dryness and itch. In summer, dandruff care is mostly about controlling oil. In winter, the dermatologists say, you also need to restore moisture and support the barrier. Very hot showers work against that goal.

## Dandruff vs dry scalp: how to tell the difference

The two look alike, but they are not the same problem, and the wrong product can make things worse. A dermatology clinic guide published on 4 November 2026 gives a useful checklist:

- **Flakes:** small, dry, white flakes suggest a dry scalp. Larger, slightly greasy, yellowish-white flakes suggest dandruff.
- **Feel:** a tight, dry scalp points to dryness. An oily or greasy scalp points to dandruff.
- **Itch:** an itch that eases after moisturizing is typical of dryness. An itch that returns even right after washing is more typical of dandruff.
- **Other areas:** flaking or redness around the eyebrows, the sides of the nose, behind the ears or **in the beard** suggests seborrheic dermatitis rather than simple dryness.

The same guide warns that heavy oiling is not a universal fix. Oil can soothe a dry scalp, but it may worsen dandruff or seborrheic dermatitis, because the yeast involved feeds on scalp oils. The American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) adds that dandruff usually improves with a dandruff shampoo, while a dry scalp often improves by switching to a gentle, non-medicated shampoo. Getting this distinction right is the first step in how to get rid of dandruff without drying the scalp further.

## When flakes point to seborrheic dermatitis

Dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis sit on a related spectrum, but they are not identical. The AAD notes that persistent or severe flakes can be a sign of seborrheic dermatitis, psoriasis, a fungal infection of the scalp or eczema, which is why a board-certified dermatologist should make the diagnosis if a standard anti-dandruff routine does not help.

The AAD explains that a dandruff shampoo can be enough for mild to moderate seborrheic dermatitis on the scalp. The aim is to clear or lessen the scale, ease the itch and help prevent flare-ups. Men with straight or wavy hair may use a dandruff shampoo two to three times a week; men with curly or tightly coiled hair may be advised to use it about once a week. Leave it on for the time the dermatologist or the bottle recommends. Once the scalp is calmer, many patients continue a dandruff shampoo once a week to help prevent the next flare.

The same guidance is useful for seborrheic dermatitis in the beard: a dermatologist may recommend shampooing the beard, the face or another affected area, not only the scalp. If one shampoo stops working as well as it once did, rotate dandruff shampoos with different active ingredients.

## The anti-dandruff ingredients that actually work

Esquire notes that the classic dandruff actives target Malassezia, a yeast on the scalp that causes flaking in people whose skin reacts to it. GQ's 2026 dandruff shampoo guide explains the main options in plain terms, and the AAD lists the ingredients to look for on the bottle:

- **Ketoconazole:** an antifungal and one of the most effective dandruff treatments. It also helps reduce overactive oil.
- **Zinc pyrithione:** targets yeast and helps loosen active flakes. Effective for most men, though less comfortable for some with eczema or psoriasis.
- **Selenium sulfide:** a strong antifungal, long used for seborrheic dermatitis.
- **Salicylic acid:** exfoliates, lifts dead skin and buildup, and tones oil. Dermatologists in the Men's Health piece say it helps both dandruff and dry scalp, but should not be overused on a sensitive scalp.
- **Sulfur:** included by the AAD among the proven over-the-counter actives.
- **Coal tar:** another AAD-listed option. Coal tar can make the scalp more sensitive to UV light, so a thinning crown still needs shade, a hat or sunscreen outdoors.
- **Climbazole:** often a gentler alternative to ketoconazole against yeast buildup, used in premium formulas such as Sachajuan's.
- **Piroctone olamine:** another antifungal used in some premium scalp shampoos, including Sachajuan's, often alongside climbazole or salicylic acid.

For a scalp that is only dry, the Men's Health experts look for humectants and barrier helpers instead: glycerin, hyaluronic acid and ceramides. In that case, a medicated shampoo may be unnecessarily harsh.

## How to use dandruff shampoo the right way

Most men rinse too fast. The AAD's dermatologist tips are simple and worth following closely:

1. **Lather on the scalp, not just the hair.** The active ingredients need contact with the skin.
2. **Let it sit.** Many dandruff shampoos work best when left on the scalp for about 5 to 10 minutes before rinsing. Follow the bottle.
3. **Match the frequency to your hair.** With fine or straight hair or an oily scalp, you may shampoo daily or every other day and use the dandruff shampoo about twice a week. With coarse, curly or coily hair, wash when needed and use the dandruff shampoo about once a week, applied only to the scalp.
4. **Rotate if it stops working.** If one shampoo stops helping, alternate between dandruff shampoos with different active ingredients.
5. **Stay with it once the flakes settle.** For seborrheic dermatitis, the AAD notes that patients often continue a dandruff shampoo once a week to help prevent flare-ups.

The AAD also points out that some formulas, such as coal tar, make the scalp more sensitive to UV light, so a thinning crown still needs protection outdoors.

## The winter routine: alternate, moisturize, protect

The Canadian Alopecia Areata Foundation shared winter scalp advice from dermatologist Dr. Jeff Donovan that suits any man with a flaky scalp. Over-shampooing can dry the scalp, and the right rhythm differs: some men can wash daily, others only every three days or once a week.

If dandruff shampoo feels too drying, he suggests combining it with a regular shampoo. In winter, when dandruff is more noticeable, he recommends using the dandruff shampoo every second or third wash. That rotation, a medicated wash on some days and a gentle hydrating shampoo on the others, is one of the most practical winter adjustments a man can make. Men with little hair can apply a moisturizer to the scalp to reduce itching, irritation and flaking. And on a sunny winter trip, a hat or SPF 30 on the scalp still matters, especially if the formula contains coal tar.

Men's Health adds a few things to avoid on a sensitive scalp: intense fragrances, menthol, heavy oils, very hot water, aggressive scrubbing and picking at flakes.

## Everyday habits that help

Mayo Clinic lists a few lifestyle points beyond the shampoo itself:

- **Manage stress.** Stress can trigger dandruff or make it worse.
- **Use fewer styling products.** Gels, sprays and pastes can build up on the scalp and make it oilier and flakier. On days you use a strong pomade, wash it out properly.
- **Massage, then rinse well.** Gently massage the scalp to loosen flakes and rinse thoroughly.
- **Get a little sun,** but not too much, and use sunscreen on your face and body.

If mild dandruff persists, Mayo Clinic suggests starting with more frequent washing using a gentle shampoo, then moving to a medicated dandruff shampoo two to three times a week.

## Don't forget the beard

Flakes under a full beard often come from the same process as on the scalp. When the skin around the eyebrows, the sides of the nose or the beard area turns red and flaky, the clinic guide above points to seborrheic dermatitis. The AAD similarly notes that a dandruff shampoo can be used on other areas a dermatologist identifies, including the beard and the face.

Many men use their dandruff shampoo on the beard skin as well, massaging it in, leaving it on for the time the bottle directs, and rinsing well, then follow with a light, non-greasy beard product. Heavy beard oils may soothe tight, dry skin, but they are less suitable when the flakes are greasy or yellowish. If the skin stays red or scaly, see a dermatologist rather than adding more oil.

## When to see a dermatologist

The dermatologists in the Men's Health piece set a clear limit: if you use a targeted shampoo for over a month without improvement, call your dermatologist. The AAD agrees that if over-the-counter products do not help, a board-certified dermatologist should make a proper diagnosis, because dryness, seborrheic dermatitis, psoriasis, eczema and allergic reactions to hair products can look similar. Severe dandruff, spreading redness or thick scale also belong in a specialist's office.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between dandruff and dry scalp?

A dry scalp sheds small, dry, white flakes and feels tight. Dandruff usually means larger, greasier, yellowish flakes and an itch that returns soon after washing. Dandruff often responds to a medicated shampoo, while a dry scalp improves with a gentle, hydrating shampoo.

### Why is dandruff worse in winter?

Cold outdoor air and heated indoor air remove moisture and weaken the scalp barrier. The scalp may then produce more oil to compensate, which can feed dandruff and make flakes more visible. Very hot showers can add to the dryness.

### How often should men use dandruff shampoo?

According to the AAD, men with straight hair or an oily scalp may use dandruff shampoo about twice a week, while men with curly or coarse hair may use it about once a week. In winter, many dermatologists suggest using it every second or third wash and a gentle shampoo in between.

### How long should I leave dandruff shampoo on my scalp?

Many dandruff shampoos work best when left on the scalp for about 5 to 10 minutes before rinsing, but always follow the instructions on the bottle. Some luxury formulas, such as Sachajuan's, ask for 1 to 2 minutes.

### Can I use dandruff shampoo on my beard?

Yes. The AAD notes that a dermatologist may recommend using a dandruff shampoo on the beard or face when those areas are involved. Massage it into the skin, leave it on as directed, rinse well and follow with a light, non-greasy product. If redness or scale persists, see a dermatologist.

### When should I see a dermatologist?

If a targeted shampoo used consistently for about a month does not improve flakes, itch or redness, see a board-certified dermatologist. The same applies if the skin around the eyebrows, nose, ears or beard is red and scaly, or if the flakes are severe.
