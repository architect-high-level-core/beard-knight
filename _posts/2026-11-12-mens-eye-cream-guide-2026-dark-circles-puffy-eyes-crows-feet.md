## Start with the right diagnosis: lines, puffiness or dark circles

GQ's 2026 eye cream test splits the problem into three, and that split is the most useful place to begin. The best eye cream for men is the one whose ingredients match your main concern, not one product asked to correct every complaint.

Fine lines come from skin losing elasticity through aging and UV damage. At the outer corners they are called crow's feet, or lateral canthal lines, and often appear first when you smile or squint. Puffiness is usually lifestyle: allergies, diet and sleep. Fluid can pool overnight, so the area looks swollen in the morning and calmer by afternoon, while lasting under-eye bags often come from weakened tissue and fat that has shifted forward. Dark circles come either from extra pigment or from blood vessels showing through the thin under-eye skin; a hollow can also cast a shadow that looks like a dark circle even when the skin itself has not changed color.

## Dark circles under the eyes: often a shadow, not a color

Mayo Clinic lists the usual causes of dark circles: genetics, fatigue, rubbing your eyes, eczema, sun exposure and normal skin changes with age. Smoking, too much alcohol and stress can make them worse. Often what looks like a dark circle is simply a shadow from puffy lids or from hollows that form with age.

Cleveland Clinic adds that as the skin loosens and thins, blood vessels become easier to see, and hollowed areas called tear troughs can cast a deeper shadow. Poor sleep can make the under-eye skin appear paler, so the vessels show through more clearly. Too much sun can trigger extra melanin, leaving a browner mark, and rubbing can swell the area and break small vessels.

Mayo's simple fixes remain a solid first step: a cold cloth or a cold spoon, eye products with caffeine, kojic acid or vitamin K, more sleep, and a broad-spectrum SPF 30 every day. Cleveland Clinic also suggests a slightly raised head at night and a cold compress. If the darkness is mainly pigment, daily sun protection and brightening care matter most. If it is mainly a hollow, no topical product will rebuild the contour.

## Puffy eyes in the morning: the eye doctor's checklist

The American Academy of Ophthalmology explains that eye bags form when the tissue around the eye weakens and fat shifts forward, and that fluid can pool there overnight. Mayo Clinic notes that allergies, smoking, a family tendency and, in some cases, other medical conditions can add to the swelling.

If the puffiness is worst when you wake and fades during the day, you are mostly looking at fluid. If the fullness stays put from morning to evening, you may be looking at a structural bag. A caffeine eye cream and a cooler night can help the first kind. They will not remove true fat pads.

The Academy's tips are practical: a cool, damp washcloth for a few minutes while you sit upright, seven to nine hours of sleep, your head raised slightly, fewer drinks before bed and less salt at dinner. Quitting smoking matters too, because it speeds up collagen loss and thins the skin under the eyes. Avoid rubbing the eyelids, and treat allergies where you can. Cleveland Clinic adds that swelling that lasts longer than a day or two, or that is painful, red or itchy, belongs with an eye-care professional.

## Crow's feet: why the outer corners line first

Cleveland Clinic describes crow's feet as small lines at the outer corners of the eyes, formed as the tiny muscles there contract when you smile, squint or frown. They usually start as lines you notice only with expression and, over time, can remain at rest. Collagen and elastin naturally decline with age; sun, diet and genetics influence how early and how deeply the lines settle.

The clinic notes that crow's feet tend to appear earlier and form deeper wrinkles in men than in women. Researchers point to behavior as one reason: men are less likely to wear sunglasses or use sunscreen. Many men first notice the lines in their thirties. Prevention starts with a broad-spectrum sunscreen of SPF 30 or higher, plus sunglasses with UV protection or a wide-brimmed hat, as Cleveland Clinic and the American Academy of Dermatology both recommend.

## The ingredients that actually earn a place in an eye cream

Cleveland Clinic dermatologist Dr. Shilpi Khetarpal's advice is to choose a formula by its ingredients, not by the weight of the jar.

### Caffeine, for temporary puffiness

Caffeine is a vasoconstrictor: it tightens blood vessels, which can make a tired under-eye area look brighter and less puffy for a while. Cleveland Clinic notes that the effect is temporary, fades when you stop, and will not correct bags that come from genetics or lid structure. Morning is the logical time to use it.

### Retinol and retinal, for lines and texture

Retinol, a form of vitamin A that stimulates collagen, is the classic ingredient for crow's feet. The eye area is thin and easily irritated, so RoC's guidance is simple: use it near the eyes only when the product says it belongs there, introduce one retinoid at a time, start every other night and wear SPF 30 or higher the next morning. Esquire's 2026 test favors retinal (retinaldehyde) in an eye cream that also carries ceramides and hyaluronic acid. Retinol can cause itching, peeling or redness when you first use it.

### Peptides, vitamin C, hyaluronic acid and niacinamide

Cleveland Clinic points to peptides for skin that looks thin or crepey, because they support collagen and elastin. Antioxidants such as vitamin C, vitamin E and ferulic acid help protect against environmental damage; vitamin C is also used as a morning brightener, beside sunscreen. Hyaluronic acid and ceramides hydrate this easily dried area, so fine lines look less sharp, though they will not fill a deep hollow. Niacinamide and kojic acid are the usual brightening choice for pigment-led circles that remain when you are rested.

## How to apply it: a morning and evening routine

Cleveland Clinic recommends a small amount, applied with a gentle touch. The ring finger works well. Pat the product around the eyes and avoid pulling or tugging the skin. Apply the eye cream before moisturizer, so the ingredients can settle into the skin. More product will not speed the result.

In the morning, cleanse gently, then use a caffeine eye cream if puffiness is the concern, or a vitamin C formula if brightness is the aim. Follow with moisturizer as needed and a broad-spectrum sunscreen of SPF 30 or higher. If a sunscreen stings, choose one better suited to the eye area rather than leaving the skin bare.

In the evening, use your richer or retinoid eye cream. If you are starting a retinol or retinal formula made for the eye area, begin every other night and keep to a single retinoid at a time. A hydrating formula with hyaluronic acid or ceramides can support the barrier if the skin feels dry. Stop ordinary products that sting or burn, as the American Academy of Dermatology advises. Ingredients aimed at fine lines often need several weeks of steady use.

## Lifestyle habits that change how the eye area looks

Sleep, salt, alcohol and the sun decide how much work an eye cream is being asked to do. The American Academy of Ophthalmology recommends seven to nine hours of sleep, with the head slightly raised, and advises limiting salt at dinner and fluids just before bed. The American Academy of Dermatology advises drinking less alcohol, using a broad-spectrum, water-resistant SPF 30 or higher every day, wearing sunglasses with UV protection, avoiding tanning, and stopping smoking, which speeds wrinkles and thins the under-eye skin.

## New in the US: bemotrizinol sunscreen for daily face protection

Sun is a major driver of fine lines and under-eye pigment, and US men finally have a new option. In June 2026 the FDA added bemotrizinol (BEMT) to its list of permitted sunscreen ingredients, the first new one in more than 25 years. It protects against both UVA and UVB, and the FDA says very little of it is absorbed through the skin. Ultra Violette launched the first US formula with it at the end of August, and Neutrogena followed in September; CeraVe and La Roche-Posay have announced their own versions.

## When a cream is not enough

Mayo Clinic notes that dark circles are usually not a medical problem. But if one eye changes on its own and gets worse over time, see a doctor. If home care does not help, a dermatologist can discuss prescription creams, lasers, chemical peels or fillers for hollows, and surgery can reduce puffy lids. Cleveland Clinic likewise lists brightening creams, peels, laser therapy, hyaluronic acid fillers, eyelid surgery and, in some cases, platelet-rich plasma among the options a clinician may consider. For deeper crow's feet, a dermatologist may also discuss a peel, a prescription retinoid or botulinum toxin injections. A good eye cream refines the look of the area; it does not fill a deep tear trough or remove true fat pads.

## FAQ

### What is the best eye cream for dark circles?

It depends on the cause. Caffeine and cold compresses help a bluish, tired look; daily SPF and brightening ingredients such as vitamin C, niacinamide or kojic acid help brown pigment; hollows need a dermatologist.

### How do I reduce puffy eyes in the morning?

Sleep seven to nine hours with your head slightly raised, use a cool cloth, and cut back on salt and drinks before bed, as the American Academy of Ophthalmology recommends. A caffeine eye cream may help fluid-related puffiness, not structural bags.

### Can men use retinol around the eyes?

Yes, if the product is made for the eye area. Start every other night, use one retinoid at a time and wear SPF 30 or higher the next morning. If redness or stinging persists, pause and speak with a dermatologist.

### How should I apply a men's eye cream?

Use a small amount. Cleveland Clinic suggests the ring finger and a gentle pat, without tugging. Apply the eye cream before moisturizer, and keep it out of the eye.

### When should I start using an eye cream?

Whenever lines, puffiness or dryness start to bother you. Daily sunscreen and good sleep are the base at any age.

### When should I see a dermatologist?

See a doctor if one eye changes on its own, if swelling is painful, red, itchy or lasts more than a day or two, or if home care does not help.
