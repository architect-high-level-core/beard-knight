A beautiful pair of leather gloves is one of the quiet signatures of a well-dressed man. They are the first thing people see when you shake hands in winter. They frame a good overcoat, and they show that you care about details few others notice. Like a fine pair of shoes, great gloves are a craft object. The best are cut by hand from carefully chosen skins, sewn with tiny, even stitches and finished with decorative "points" on the back.

This guide explains the leathers, linings and stitching that separate ordinary gloves from exceptional ones, and shows how to measure your size properly. It looks at which famous glovemakers actually offer bespoke or made-to-measure work (fewer than you might think). It ends with care advice and how to wear gloves with a coat.

> **Key takeaways**
> - **Leather decides the feel.** **Peccary** is the most prized, soft and durable. **Deerskin** is rugged and supple. **Hair sheep** is smooth, strong and elastic. **Lambskin nappa** is the most delicate and refined.
> - **Lining decides the warmth.** Dents ranks natural skins as warmest, followed by **cashmere**, wool, **silk** and **unlined**.
> - **Stitching is a mark of quality.** Hidden **inseam** stitching looks sleek; an exposed **prix** seam is a popular, sporty choice for men. **Hand-sewn** gloves and **three-point** backs signal top craftsmanship.
> - **Size = palm circumference in inches.** Measure around the widest part of your dominant hand, excluding the thumb.
> - **True bespoke is rare.** Of the makers we checked, only **Thomas Riemer** in Vienna is described as doing true bespoke work. **Dents** states plainly that it does not make bespoke gloves.
> - **Care is simple:** dry wet gloves flat at room temperature, never on a radiator, and condition them occasionally.

## Why Leather Gloves Still Matter

Gloves have been a symbol of status for centuries. Their craft survives in a handful of historic centres: Worcester and Warminster in England, Millau in France, Naples in Italy and Vienna in Austria. Dents, founded in 1777 by John Dent in Wood Street, Worcester, still makes its Heritage Collection in its factory in Warminster.

Original article: [Our Story – Dents](https://www.dentsgloves.com/pages/our-story)

Dents explains that a pair of its gloves can go through **up to 32 steps**, from selecting and stretching the skin to cutting, stitching and finishing. That level of handwork is why a fine glove fits like a second skin and lasts for years.

Original article: [Dents Guide to Buying Gloves – Dents](https://www.dentsgloves.com/pages/dents-guide-to-buying-gloves)

## The Leathers: From Nappa to Peccary

| Leather | Character | Feel and durability | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lambskin / nappa** | Smooth, fine grain, slight sheen | Very soft, thin, elegant; less rugged | City, evening, formal coats |
| **Hair sheep** | Smooth, tight grain | Strong, elastic, supple; often called the best glove leather | Everyday luxury, driving |
| **Deerskin** | Visible, rugged grain | Soft, stretchy, very durable; moulds to the hand | Country coats, tweed, everyday |
| **Peccary** | Distinctive three-dot pore pattern | Extremely soft, supple and durable | The connoisseur's choice |

### Lambskin and Nappa

Nappa usually describes soft, full-grain sheep or lamb leather. It gives the sleekest, most refined look and is the classic choice with a dark overcoat or evening wear. It is thinner and more delicate than the other leathers, so treat it with care.

### Hair Sheep

Dents explains that hair sheep leather comes from sheep that grow **fine hair rather than wool**, which leaves the leather smooth. On its product pages Dents calls it "generally acknowledged as the best leather for gloves" for its strength and elasticity, and it is supple enough for driving gloves.

### Deerskin

According to Dents, deerskin has the strength and elasticity of hair sheep but a more **rugged appearance due to the grain**; it is warm, breathable and soft. It is perfect with tweed, flannel and country coats.

### Peccary

Peccary is the aristocrat of glove leathers. Dents describes it as a beautiful, soft and durable leather "originating from wild boars" (peccaries are boar-like animals of the Americas), rare and prized because it requires **specialist cutting and hand sewing**. Its grain shows a distinctive three-pore pattern, and it is remarkably soft and supple. Because the skins are rare, peccary gloves are the most expensive in most makers' collections. Causse notes that shipping peccary gloves outside the European Union may take extra time.

## The Linings: Cashmere, Silk or Unlined?

Dents ranks linings from warmest to coolest as follows: **natural skins (such as sheepskin) > cashmere > wool > silk > unlined**.

Original article: [FAQs – Dents](https://www.dentsgloves.com/pages/faqs)

| Lining | Warmth | Fit | When to wear |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cashmere** | Very warm, light | Slightly fuller; size carefully | Deep winter, with overcoats |
| **Wool** | Warm | Similar to cashmere | Everyday winter |
| **Silk** | Light; Dents notes it helps regulate temperature | Close, elegant fit | Autumn and spring, formal wear |
| **Unlined** | Least warm | Closest, most tactile fit | Driving, mild weather, purists |

Many connoisseurs own two pairs: **unlined or silk-lined** for autumn and **cashmere-lined** for winter. An unlined peccary glove is the ultimate expression of the craft, because there is nothing to hide imperfect stitching.

## Stitching and Construction: Reading the Details

| Term | What it means | Look |
|---|---|---|
| **Inseam** | Seams sewn inside and hidden | Sleek, dressy |
| **Prix seam** | Exposed seam (Dents: more popular in men's gloves) | Crisp, sporty |
| **Pique** | One edge laid over the other and stitched on the outside | Neat, dressy, visible |
| **Whip stitch / brosser** | Visible stitch wrapped over the edge | Casual, handcrafted |
| **Hand-sewn** | Stitched entirely by hand | Superior shaping and finish |
| **Points** | Three lines (sometimes one) of decorative stitching on the back | The classic gentleman's glove |
| **Fourchettes** | Inside panels on the fingers | Better finger shape and movement |
| **Quirks** | Small diamond-shaped pieces hand-sewn at the base of the fingers | Better fit, a mark of quality |
| **Vent** | V-shaped cut on the palm or back at the wrist | Better fit around the wrist |
| **Strap and roller** | Strap for adjusting the fit around the wrist | Heritage detail |

Original article: [Dents Guide to Buying Gloves – Dents](https://www.dentsgloves.com/pages/dents-guide-to-buying-gloves)

"Three-point" gloves feature three lines of stitching on the back of the hand, a hallmark of traditional craftsmanship. Hand-sewn gloves take far longer to make. Thomas Riemer in Vienna notes that an experienced seamstress makes only **three pairs of entirely hand-sewn gloves a day**.

Original article: [Making-of – Thomas Riemer Handschuhe](https://www.tr-handschuhe.at/en/pages/making-of)

## How to Measure Your Glove Size

Glove sizes are traditionally given in inches:

1. Take a soft tape measure.
2. Wrap it around the **widest part of your dominant hand's palm**, just below the knuckles, **excluding the thumb**.
3. Keep your hand flat and relaxed, fingers together.
4. The measurement **in inches** is your size. Thomas Riemer, for example, offers full and half sizes.

Original article: [Sizing Guide – Thomas Riemer Handschuhe](https://www.tr-handschuhe.at/en/pages/sizing-guide)

| Palm circumference (cm) | Approx. inches | Glove size |
|---|---|---|
| 19.0 | 7.5 | 7½ |
| 20.3 | 8.0 | 8 |
| 21.6 | 8.5 | 8½ |
| 22.9 | 9.0 | 9 |
| 24.1 | 9.5 | 9½ |
| 25.4 | 10.0 | 10 |

Some Neapolitan makers use a different calculation. Omega's size page divides the palm circumference in centimetres by **2.7** (the old French inch), so always check each maker's own chart.

Original article: [Sizes – Omega Gloves](https://www.omegasrl.com/pages/sizes)

**Fit tip:** new leather gloves should feel **snug**, like a second skin. Leather relaxes with wear, and Thomas Riemer advises that gloves should be snug when new. Cashmere-lined gloves feel fuller than unlined ones in the same size.

### Thomas Riemer, Vienna

Thomas Riemer's father, Erwin, co-founded the Austrian glove company RIKA in the 1960s, which outfitted athletes at the 1964 Innsbruck Olympics. Thomas later took over Vienna's oldest glove shop, founded in 1845. His gloves were first made in Hungarian factories; in 2009 he opened his own workshop in Hungary, and experienced seamstresses sew the hand-sewn models. In a trunk-show announcement, Julius Boutique describes Riemer's "dedication to true bespoke work", with hands "measured in exacting detail".

Original article: [Our Story – Thomas Riemer Handschuhe](https://www.tr-handschuhe.at/en/pages/our-story)

Original article: [Trunk Show: Crockett & Jones and Thomas Riemer Gloves – Julius Boutique](https://julius-boutique.com/pages/trunkshow-crockett-jones-thomas-riemer-gloves)

### Dents, England

The best-known English glovemaker, founded in 1777. Dents is admirably clear: "Unfortunately, we are unable to make bespoke gloves or provide any alterations on our products." Its ready-to-wear Heritage Collection, made in Britain, is nonetheless an excellent benchmark.

### Causse, Millau

Founded in 1892 in Millau, the historic capital of French glovemaking, Causse has passed through four generations. Around forty artisans work in workshops designed by architect Jean-Michel Wilmotte. Since 2012 it has been part of CHANEL's Maisons d'art. It holds the French Entreprise du Patrimoine Vivant label.

Original article: [The House of Causse Gantier – Causse](https://www.caussegantier.com/en/pages/house-of-causse-gantier)

### Sermoneta, Rome

Founded by Giorgio Sermoneta in the 1960s, the Rome house is known for its coloured gloves. Its tailoring page describes a paper pattern, hand cutting and a thumb shaped separately, a craft it has practised for over fifty years.

Original article: [La Sartoria – Sermoneta Gloves](https://www.sermonetagloves.it/en/pages/la-sartoria)

### Merola, Omega and the Neapolitan Tradition

Naples is the heart of Italian glovemaking. **Merola**, based in Rome, makes all its gloves in Naples, hand-cut and hand-stitched. **Omega** was founded in 1923 by Gennaro Squillace. It is now led by fifth-generation maître gantier Alberto Squillace and describes **25 production stages by hand**. It works in plongé lamb, peccary, carpincho and deer, with silk and Biella cashmere linings, in the glovemaking tradition of Naples' Sanità district.

Original article: [History – Merola Gloves](https://www.merolagloves.it/history/)

Original article: [About – Omega Gloves](https://www.omegasrl.com/pages/omega-srl-about)

**Our advice:** if you want true bespoke, contact the maker directly, ask whether they will take your measurements and adjust the pattern, and ask how many fittings are included.

## How to Care for Leather Gloves

Dents recommends a few simple rules:

- **If gloves get wet, dry them flat on a towel at room temperature.**
- **Never** dry them on a radiator or heater, in a tumble dryer or in direct sunlight. Keep them away from air conditioning too.
- **Put them on while they are still slightly damp** and flex your hands to "massage" the leather back into shape.
- **Apply a small dab of leather conditioner** occasionally to keep the leather supple.

Original article: [How to Care for Leather Gloves – Dents](https://us.dentsgloves.com/pages/how-to-care-for-leather-gloves)

Further good habits:

- **Remove gloves finger by finger**, not by pulling the cuff, to protect the seams.
- **Store them flat**, not balled up in a pocket, in a breathable bag.
- **Keep fragrance, hand cream and beard oil off the leather.** Apply hand cream and beard oil well before putting on gloves; oils can stain light leathers.

## How to Wear Leather Gloves With a Coat

| Coat | Glove | Colour pairing |
|---|---|---|
| **Navy or charcoal overcoat** | Unlined or silk-lined nappa or hair sheep, three-point | Black or dark brown |
| **Camel coat** | Peccary or deerskin, cashmere-lined | Tan, cognac or chocolate |
| **Tweed or country coat** | Deerskin with prix seams | Tan or brown |
| **Black evening coat** | Fine black nappa, inseam | Black only |
| **Leather jacket or shearling** | Deerskin or peccary | Brown or black matched to the jacket |

Style rules worth knowing:

- **Match the glove to your shoes and belt**, not necessarily to the coat.
- **Brown is more versatile than black** for daytime. Black is the formal choice.
- **The cuff should disappear under the coat sleeve.** Gloves that stop short at the wrist look mean.
- **Colour is allowed.** Bottle green, burgundy or navy gloves with a grey coat are a sophisticated touch.

## FAQ

### What is the best leather for gloves?

Hair sheep is widely considered the best all-round glove leather for its strength and elasticity. Peccary is the most luxurious and durable. Deerskin is more rugged, and lambskin nappa is the most refined but also the most delicate.

### Are cashmere-lined gloves worth it?

Yes, for winter. Cashmere is one of the warmest linings while staying light. Dents ranks it second only to natural sheepskin. For autumn or driving, unlined or silk-lined gloves feel more tactile.

### How do I know my glove size?

Measure around the widest part of the palm of your dominant hand, excluding the thumb. The figure in inches is your glove size. Some Neapolitan makers use a centimetre-based formula, so check the maker's chart.

### Should leather gloves be tight?

They should be snug, not painful. Leather relaxes with wear, so a new pair should feel close-fitting, especially unlined gloves.

### Can I get bespoke gloves from Dents?

No. Dents states on its FAQ page that it is unable to make bespoke gloves or provide alterations. Thomas Riemer in Vienna is described by stockists as doing true bespoke work.

### Why are peccary gloves so expensive?

Peccary skins are rare, need specialist cutting and are often sewn by hand. The result is very soft, very durable leather with a distinctive grain.

### How do I clean leather gloves?

Spot-clean gently with a slightly damp cloth, let them dry naturally at room temperature and apply a small amount of leather conditioner. Avoid heat and washing machines unless the maker says the gloves are washable.

## The Bottom Line

Great gloves are a small investment that pays off every winter. Choose the leather for the life you lead: nappa for the city, hair sheep for everyday, deerskin for the country and peccary for a lifetime. Choose cashmere for deep cold and silk or unlined for elegance. Look for hidden inseams or crisp prix seams, three points and hand-sewn details.

Measure your hand carefully, and if you want true bespoke, ask the maker directly. Of the houses we checked, Thomas Riemer in Vienna is the one publicly described as offering it. Then look after them: dry them gently, condition them occasionally and wear them with a coat that deserves them.
