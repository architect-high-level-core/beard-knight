Today is November 1, and that means one thing on the upper lip of men around the world: Movember 2026 has begun. Since 2003 the mustache has been the symbol of a serious idea, getting men to talk about their health, from prostate and testicular cancer to mental health. This guide covers both sides of the month: the mustache styles worth growing in 2026, how to grow and groom a mustache with real polish, and the men's health checks that matter far more than any whisker.

## 1. What Movember 2026 Is About: The "Growmitment"

Movember started in 2003 when two friends in a Melbourne pub challenged their mates to grow a mustache for men's health; thirty of them said yes. Twenty-three years later, the 2026 campaign gives that commitment a new name, the "Growmitment." You pick one of four ways to take part: **Grow** a mustache, **Move** 60 kilometers (or 60 miles on the US site) over the month, **Host** a fundraising event, or **Mo Your Own Way** with a personal challenge. Then you challenge a friend, a colleague or your whole team to join you.

The numbers behind it are serious. Movember says it has raised more than $1 billion and funded over 1,320 programs in more than 20 countries. The money goes to three areas: mental health and suicide prevention, prostate cancer and testicular cancer. The Move challenge has a sobering meaning: 60 kilometers for the 60 men the world loses to suicide every hour.

**Why it matters:** a mustache is a conversation starter. The real goal is the conversations, and the checkups, that follow.

## 2. The Best Mustache Styles for 2026: Chevron, Pencil and Handlebar

If you are growing a mustache for the first time, the experts agree on where to start: the **chevron**. It is thick and full, follows the natural line of the upper lip in a soft inverted V and stops at the corners of the mouth. The Beard Club calls it "the undisputed king of 2026 mustache styles," and it is the easiest to grow because it simply follows nature. It suits oval, square and long faces best.

The **pencil mustache** is the refined alternative: a thin, precise line just above the lip. A London barber interviewed by the BBC says it works well if your hair grows lighter or patchier, but it needs regular trimming to keep the line sharp. The **handlebar**, with longer ends that curl upward, is the statement style. It needs length at the sides, so a full handlebar usually takes longer than one month.

**How long does it take to grow a mustache?** Most styles need 4 to 8 weeks to reach a workable length. Mustache hair grows roughly 0.4 mm per day, or about half an inch per month, depending on genetics, age and health. That means Movember is the start of a good mustache, not the finish line.

## 3. How to Grow a Mustache Without Ruining It

The Movember rule is simple: start clean-shaven on November 1. After that, patience is everything. The barber's advice is to let everything grow for the first couple of weeks, even areas you don't plan to keep. This shows you your natural growth pattern and where your hair is densest. Only then should you start defining the mustache and shaving the cheeks and chin again.

The biggest mistake, he says, is trimming straight across too early. It can ruin the natural shape and make the mustache look thin or uneven. "You can always take more off but you can't put it back on." The second week is the awkward stage, when density is still uneven. Don't shave it off in frustration; give the slower hairs time to catch up.

**Grooming habit:** comb your mustache every day. Just like the hair on your head, daily combing trains the direction of growth and keeps it tidy while it fills in.

## 4. Mustache Grooming Like a Barber: The Peak and the Points

GQ's barber guide explains something most men never think about: every mustache has two parts. The **peak** is the upper row of hairs, which a barber keeps shorter and neater so it doesn't add bulk. The **points** are the longer hairs at the ends and sides, which are the easiest to style because they bend to the shape you give them.

For trimming, comb the hair downward first to catch uneven lengths, then work slowly with small, sharp barber scissors or a precision trimmer. Trim the lower edge so hairs don't sit in your mouth, but don't thin out the center of a chevron, because density is what makes it look expensive. Once you have a few weeks of growth, the mustache becomes much easier to shape.

## 5. Mustache Wax and Oil: Hold, Shape and Comfort

A good mustache wax is the one product every serious mustache needs. It holds the shape, tames stray hairs and lets you lift the points of a handlebar. GQ's barbers recommend a quality wax plus a dedicated facial-hair comb. The BBC's barber adds a tip that is especially useful in winter: a touch of mustache or beard oil softens the hair and helps prevent irritation around the lips.

The right routine is simple. In the morning, comb the mustache down, warm a tiny amount of wax between your fingertips and work it from the center outward toward the ends. At night, a drop of oil keeps the hair soft and the skin underneath comfortable. If your mustache comes in lighter or patchier than your beard, the BBC's barber notes that semi-permanent tints made for facial hair can give a fuller, natural look.

## 6. The Prostate Check: When to Talk About a PSA Test

This is the part of Movember that matters most. According to the American Cancer Society, prostate cancer is the most common cancer in American men after skin cancer, and about 1 in 8 men will be diagnosed in their lifetime. The ACS recommends that men make an informed decision with their doctor about screening, and says that conversation should happen:

- at **age 50** for men at average risk,
- at **age 45** for men at higher risk, including African American men and men whose father or brother was diagnosed before 65,
- at **age 40** for men at even higher risk, such as those with more than one close relative diagnosed young or men who carry a BRCA mutation.

Men who choose screening get a PSA blood test. The ACS says men with a PSA below 2.5 ng/mL may only need retesting every two years, while a level of 2.5 or higher calls for yearly testing. The American Urological Association's 2026 guideline adds that doctors may offer a baseline PSA test between ages 45 and 50, and should offer regular screening every two to four years to men aged 50 to 69.

**The takeaway:** if you are over 45, or have a family history, use Movember as the reason to book that conversation with your doctor.

## 7. Testicular Cancer: The Self-Check Young Men Skip

Prostate cancer is mostly a concern for older men, but Movember points out that testicular cancer is the number one cancer among young men, and most young men don't know it. Movember's "Know Thy Nuts" campaign encourages men to know their risk, know what is normal for their body, and act quickly if something feels different.

The check itself takes a minute. MedlinePlus, the US National Library of Medicine's health site, describes a testicular self-exam best done during or after a warm shower, when the skin is relaxed: gently roll each testicle between thumb and fingers and feel for any hard lump, swelling or change in size or shape. Anything new or unusual is a reason to see a doctor promptly, not to wait and see.

## FAQ: Movember and Mustache Grooming

### What is Movember 2026?

Movember is a global men's health campaign that runs every November. In 2026, participants make a "Growmitment": grow a mustache, move 60 km, host an event or set their own challenge, raising funds for prostate cancer, testicular cancer and men's mental health.

### How long does it take to grow a mustache for Movember?

Most mustache styles need 4 to 8 weeks to reach a workable length. Facial hair grows about half an inch per month, so a full handlebar usually takes longer than November.

### What is the easiest mustache style to grow?

The chevron is the easiest mustache for beginners. It follows the natural shape of the upper lip, needs little styling and works especially well with thicker growth.

### At what age should men talk to a doctor about prostate cancer screening?

The American Cancer Society recommends the screening conversation at 50 for average-risk men, at 45 for higher-risk men, including African American men and those with a father or brother diagnosed before 65, and at 40 for men at even higher risk.
