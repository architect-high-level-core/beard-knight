November is the month when a good coat stops being enough. The details that finish a cold-weather look are the ones closest to your hands and your face: a pair of well-cut leather gloves and a soft cashmere scarf. In this guide to men's leather gloves and cashmere scarves for 2026, we cover the season's colors, how gloves should really fit, what separates fine leather from the rest, and the scarf knots that work with a buttoned overcoat.

## 1. The 2026 Color Story: Burgundy, Damson and Olive

Black and brown gloves will never go out of style, but this season the luxury glove makers are pushing richer tones. British glove maker Dents names deep burgundy and softer damson as the defining reds of fall/winter 2026, paired with plain cashmere scarves in the same wine shades. The second big story is what Dents calls the return of "manorcore": countryside checks and greens, with olive and forest green leather and heritage check cashmere scarves.

For men, Dents points to its Bruton glove in olive, which combines supple leather with quilted waxed cotton, a nod to classic British country dressing. The easy way to wear the trend is to let one piece carry the color: a burgundy scarf with dark brown gloves, or olive gloves with a charcoal or camel scarf.

**Why it matters:** Accessories are the lowest-risk way to try a new color. A burgundy scarf or olive gloves refresh a navy or charcoal coat you already own.

## 2. Jewel Tones, Buttery Leather and the Return of the Tie

Harrods' guide to menswear for autumn/winter 2026 confirms the bigger picture. The winter palette is still built on black, navy and grey, but it is "shot through with rich jewel tones," with deep aubergine named the most popular shade on the Milan and Paris runways, next to mustard and emerald green. The same richness shows up in texture: plush velvet, the finest cashmere and soft, buttery leather.

Harrods also notes that the tie was "everywhere" this season, as tailoring returns to a relaxed, 1930s-inspired line. That matters for accessories: a refined cashmere scarf and slim leather gloves are the natural companions of a suit-and-tie look under an overcoat.

**Why it matters:** If you are building a winter wardrobe around tailoring, your gloves and scarf should match its level. Cashmere and fine leather are part of the look, not an afterthought.

## 4. How Should Men's Leather Gloves Fit?

Gear Patrol's guide to the best winter gloves for men sums up the right fit in one word: snug. Not too tight, but close enough that you can comfortably make a fist without extra material hanging past your fingertips. The magazine compares good leather gloves to a leather jacket for your hands, and says the best ones should cover most or all of your wrist to keep out the cold.

To find your size, InStyle quotes Andrew Lawson, director of glove maker Cornelia James: measure around the widest part of your hand, excluding the thumb, with a soft tape. The result in inches is your glove size. If you are between sizes, round down, because a leather glove should feel a little tight on the first wear and will settle to your hand.

**Why it matters:** Loose gloves look cheap and keep you colder. A precise fit is what makes a pair of leather gloves look tailored.

Original article: [Gear Patrol: The Best Winter Gloves for Men](https://www.gearpatrol.com/style/g38256829/gloves-for-men/)

## 5. How to Spot Quality Leather (and Keep It That Way)

InStyle's 2026 guide to the best leather gloves gathers clear advice from glove makers. Look for full-grain leather, the top layer of the hide and the most durable. Nappa sheep leather, which is treated to be soft and crease-resistant, and hairsheep leather, which is smooth and flexible, are named as two excellent choices. Italian leather is singled out as a reliable sign of quality.

Then check the stitching. Straight, even seams mean the gloves were not rushed and will last longer, and they also suggest that all pieces were cut from the same skin, so the color ages evenly. For care, the guide suggests wiping the leather with a cloth and soapy water at the end of the season and gently hand-washing a cashmere lining after turning the gloves inside out.

**Why it matters:** The best gloves are made, not just designed. Leather type, lining and stitching tell you more than a logo.

## 7. How to Tie a Scarf With an Overcoat: The Parisian Knot

The most useful scarf knot for a buttoned overcoat is the Parisian knot, also called the European loop. Tailor Guide explains it in four steps: fold the scarf in half end to end, place it around the back of your neck with the loop on one side, pull both loose ends through the loop, and slide it up until it is as snug as you like. It sits neatly at the throat and fills the neck of the coat without bulk.

Mr Porter adds that the Parisian knot is easy, very warm and comes off in seconds, but because it doubles the length, it works best with a fine-wool scarf of more than 180 cm (about 70 inches). With an open coat, the simple drape is the most formal option; with a buttoned or double-breasted coat, choose a knot.

**Why it matters:** The right knot depends on the coat. A long, fine cashmere scarf gives you every option.

## FAQ: Men's Leather Gloves and Cashmere Scarves

### How do I know my glove size?

Measure around the widest part of your hand, excluding the thumb, with a soft tape. The number in inches is your glove size. If you are between sizes, glove makers advise rounding down, because leather gloves stretch slightly with wear.

### How tight should leather gloves be?

Snug but not tight. You should be able to make a fist comfortably, with no extra material past your fingertips, and the glove should cover most of your wrist.

### What is the best leather for men's gloves?

Full-grain leather is the most durable. Nappa sheep leather and hairsheep leather are both excellent for dress gloves because they are soft, smooth and flexible. Straight, even stitching is a sign of good construction.

### What is the best way to tie a scarf with an overcoat?

For a buttoned overcoat, use the Parisian knot: fold the scarf in half, put it around your neck and pull both ends through the loop. It works best with a fine scarf of about 180 cm (70 inches). With an open coat, a simple drape looks the most formal.
