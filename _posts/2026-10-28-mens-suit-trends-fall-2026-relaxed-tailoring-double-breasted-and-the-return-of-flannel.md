The suit is back, but not the stiff, shrunken one from a decade ago. Men's suit trends for fall 2026 point to softer shoulders, fuller trousers, lower double-breasted closures and cloth you want to touch: flannel, corduroy and velvet. Here is what the runways, the best tailors and the leading menswear editors say about dressing well in tailoring this fall and winter, beard included.

**Quick take**

- Relaxed, soft tailoring has replaced the slim, shrunken suit.
- The low-buttoning double-breasted jacket is the suit of the season.
- Grey flannel is everywhere again, from Prada to Paris.
- Brown, taupe and greige are the interesting alternatives to navy.
- Corduroy and velvet bring texture to tailoring without looking stuffy.
- A sharp suit needs a beard with clean lines, not maximum volume.

## 1. Relaxed Tailoring Replaces the Shrunken Suit

The biggest shift in suit trends for 2026 is the silhouette. GQ's tailoring experts point to natural shoulders, a fuller chest, longer jackets and high-rise, pleated trousers with a straighter, wider leg. New York designer Todd Snyder summed it up for GQ: twenty years ago it was all about the shrunken suit, and now tailoring "harkens back to the '80s and '90s, with wider legs, not quite as fitted, and not shrunken at all."

Relaxed does not mean sloppy. The shoulder still has to fit, the sleeve still has to show a little shirt cuff, and the trousers should fall cleanly onto the shoe. Think of soft tailoring as comfort with intention: a suit you can sit, travel and dine in, cut with enough room to move.

## 2. The Low Double-Breasted Suit Is the Suit of the Season

The double-breasted jacket has got its groove back, and the trick is a lower buttoning point. Instead of fastening at the natural waist, the "low DB" closes slightly below it, which lengthens the lapels, draws the eye up to the shoulders and makes the jacket feel relaxed rather than armored. British designer Scott Fraser Simpson says double-breasted suits now make up around 75 percent of his tailoring sales, many in brown, taupe, putty and dove grey.

Mr Porter buying director Daniel Todd names Brunello Cucinelli, Saman Amel, Lemaire and Dries Van Noten as the best exponents of the look. His shopping advice for a modern double-breasted suit: soft construction, minimal padding, cloth with movement and drape, a wider peak lapel and a slightly longer jacket. Keep the styling quiet, for example with a fine crewneck knit and high-waisted pleated trousers.

## 3. Grey Flannel Is Back on the Runway and in the Office

Grey flannel was once the uniform of the mid-century man, and for fall 2026 it is everywhere again. At the Fall 2026 menswear shows, grey wool appeared in textured suits at Prada, flecked and charcoal suits at Paul Smith, a double-breasted grey suit opening the Ami show in Paris and grey peak-lapel jackets at Dior. The new grey suit is drapey and relaxed, closer to '90s Armani than to Mad Men.

London tailor Maximilian Mogg explains the appeal of a flannel suit: it is soft without being flimsy and elegant without looking overly polished. "Once somebody has spent an autumn or winter in a proper flannel suit, it becomes very difficult to convince him that he should wear anything else," he says. Wear it with a white shirt and tie for the office, then swap the tie for a roll-neck or knit polo in the evening.

## 4. Brown Suits: Is Brown the New Navy?

Brown keeps appearing in fall-winter 2026 tailoring, from chocolate and espresso to taupe and greige. Saint Laurent showed drapey double-breasted jackets in midnight, taupe and greige, and tailors report that the long-predicted comeback of the brown suit has finally arrived. Brown is warm, flattering on many complexions and far less expected than the default navy suit.

Brown also works beautifully with a beard, especially a darker or salt-and-pepper one, because it picks up the warm tones in facial hair. Pair a brown suit with a white, cream or pale blue shirt and dark brown suede shoes. If a full brown suit feels like a big step, start with a brown flannel or camel hair jacket worn with grey trousers.

## 5. The Corduroy Suit Grows Up

Robb Report makes a convincing case for the corduroy suit this month. At Pitti Uomo and Milan Men's Fashion Week, Massimo Alba, Brett Johnson, Cesare Attolini and Brunello Cucinelli all showed corduroy in tailored form, including a fine-wale double-breasted grey suit at Cucinelli with ribs in two subtly different shades. Today's luxury corduroy is supple and rich, an "everyman's velvet" rather than the stiff trousers of your school days.

The appeal is that texture softens formality. A corduroy suit has the structure of tailoring without making you look like a company man. Dress it up with a tie or a cashmere sweater, or wear it down with a sweatshirt; Robb Report's John Vorwald wears his with everything from derbies to combat boots.

## 6. Velvet Suiting for Evenings and the Holidays

GQ named velvety suiting one of the top fall-winter 2026 menswear trends, after Giorgio Armani, Canali and Amiri all showed it in drapey cuts. Armani in particular balanced the house's loose, bookish pleats of the early '90s with a grungier attitude from the same era. With the holiday season ahead, a velvet suit or dinner jacket in midnight, bottle green or deep brown is the most elegant alternative to a classic tuxedo.

Keep everything else simple. A velvet suit already has presence, so pair it with a crisp shirt or a fine black roll-neck and polished black or dark brown shoes.

Original article: [GQ](https://www.gq.com/story/the-9-fall-winter-2026-menswear-trends-to-try-right-now)

## 7. The Formal Parka: A Practical Way to Wear a Suit in Winter

GQ also spotted a surprisingly practical trend: the formal parka worn over tailoring. Ralph Lauren paired a full tuxedo with mountain boots and a thick silver parka, and Brunello Cucinelli showed an elegant grey pinstripe suit under a rugged navy parka. For American winters from Chicago to Boston, this may be the most useful idea of the season.

The overcoat remains the classic choice over a suit. But a well-cut parka in a dark, matte fabric, long enough to cover the jacket, is now an accepted way to protect fine tailoring on the coldest days.

Original article: [GQ](https://www.gq.com/story/the-9-fall-winter-2026-menswear-trends-to-try-right-now)

## 8. How to Wear a Suit With a Beard

A sharp suit and a beard go together perfectly, as long as the proportions are right. GQ's longtime advice: when a beard gets too bushy, it softens the jawline and puffs up the edges of the face, which works against sharp tailoring. Aim for facial hair that matches the suit, with a clean neckline and defined cheek lines. Your hair and beard should not compete; keep one shorter and more conventional than the other.

The tie matters too. A chunky knot adds width to the neck and makes a full beard look heavier. With relaxed fall 2026 tailoring, a slimmer knot, a knit tie or an open collar with fine knitwear often looks best with a beard. Shape the beard with a little balm before you dress, and check the neckline with your shirt collar buttoned.

## FAQ

### What are the biggest men's suit trends for fall 2026?

The biggest men's suit trends for fall 2026 are relaxed, soft tailoring with natural shoulders and high-rise pleated trousers, the low-buttoning double-breasted jacket, grey flannel suits, brown and taupe suiting, and textured fabrics such as corduroy and velvet.

### Is the double-breasted suit back in style in 2026?

Yes. The double-breasted suit is one of the defining looks of fall-winter 2026. The modern version buttons slightly lower, has soft construction with minimal padding, a wider peak lapel and a slightly longer jacket, worn with fuller trousers.

### Is a flannel suit good for winter?

Yes. Wool flannel is one of the best suit fabrics for fall and winter because it is warm, soft and forgiving, and it looks better as the day goes on. A grey flannel suit can be worn formally with a shirt and tie or casually with knitwear.

### How should a beard look with a suit?

A beard looks best with a suit when it is groomed and proportionate: a clean neckline, defined cheek lines and no excess bulk that softens the jaw. Keep either your hair or your beard more conventional, and choose a slimmer tie knot or knit tie with a full beard.
