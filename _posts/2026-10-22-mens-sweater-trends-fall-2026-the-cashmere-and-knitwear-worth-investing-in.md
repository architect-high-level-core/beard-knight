There is a reason autumn is called sweater weather and not sweatshirt weather. A fine knit does more for a man's wardrobe in October than almost any other single piece: it warms, it layers, it softens tailoring and it sharpens denim. This fall, the **men's sweater trends of 2026** are moving beyond the anonymous navy crewneck toward V-necks, collared knits, pattern-rich statement pieces and richer colors.

Below are the knitwear trends worth your money this season, what the runways and the American style press are saying, how to tell good cashmere from merely soft cashmere, and how to make a great sweater last for years.

> **Key takeaways**
> - **The V-neck is back.** GQ calls it the '90s heartthrob sweater, best worn over a crewneck tee or a crisp button-up.
> - **Knits are getting dressier.** Knit polos and collared cardigans are a smart alternative to a shirt and tie.
> - **The sweater is the main event.** Fall 2026 statement knits draw on Fair Isle, Nordic and windowpane patterns, without logos.
> - **Chocolate brown is the color.** Neutrals stay, but cocoa and jewel tones are the updates.
> - **Cashmere is an investment.** Check the fiber content, feel for bounce, then hand-wash, dry flat and store folded.

## 1. The V-Neck Sweater Makes Its Comeback

For years the V-neck was the sweater nobody admitted to owning. That has changed. GQ's fall 2026 sweater guide names the "'90s heartthrob V-neck" as one of the season's key knits, riding the same wave of '90s styling that is bringing back relaxed jeans and facial hair. The magazine suggests wearing one over a simple crewneck tee, which instantly makes a plain outfit look more considered. For a preppier take, layer it over a crisp button-up, or wear it with a flannel shirt for a weekend look.

GQ points to lightweight versions from J.Crew and Todd Snyder, and to heavier options such as the Irish lambswool knits from &Daughter and The Row's thick, wide-rib cashmere. For a man with a beard, the V-neck has a useful side effect: the open neckline frames the jaw and keeps a full beard from bunching against a high collar.

**How to wear it:** keep the V moderate, not deep. Wear a white or grey tee underneath, or a light oxford shirt with the collar outside the sweater. Finish with grey flannel trousers or dark denim and suede loafers.

## 2. Knit Polos and Collared Cardigans: The Dressier Sweater

The broader move toward sharper dressing has also reached knitwear. GQ notes that soft knit polos and collared cardigans are getting "a slightly dressier treatment" this season. You can keep it classic with a relaxed knit polo in navy or brown, or go bold with a patterned piece, such as Sacai's chevron design.

Robb Report adds an important voice from retail. Jian DeLeon, men's fashion director at Nordstrom, says that with a renewed focus on Ivy style and elegant silhouettes, knitwear has become "an essential way to toe the line between dressier looks and more casual ones." In his words, "smart knitwear easily pinch-hits for a shirt and tie." That is good news for the man who wants to look professional without wearing a suit every day.

**How to wear it:** a fine-gauge knit polo under a navy or charcoal blazer is the modern replacement for a dress shirt and tie at many offices and dinners. A collared cardigan works like a soft jacket over a tee or a thin turtleneck.

## 3. Statement Knits: Fair Isle, Nordic and Windowpane Patterns

The biggest shift in men's knitwear this fall is attitude. Robb Report's Jon Roth writes that designers now argue the sweater, long used as a quiet layer under tailoring and outerwear, "can also be the main attraction." What stands out is what these sweaters do not have: none of the standout pieces in the magazine's edit relies on logos, slogans or novelty graphics.

Instead, the luxury houses reinterpret traditional knit patterns. Louis Vuitton shows a deconstructed Nordic pattern that seems to dissolve down the body. Canali offers a bold windowpane check with an almost marled depth, created with a special two-ply knitting technique. Brunello Cucinelli combines Fair Isle and Scandinavian elements into a motif unique to the house.

GQ sees the same energy from a different angle. It mentions the chunky fisherman sweater, the return of American prep and collegiate-inspired knits that look like they belong on a 1960s football sideline.

**How to wear it:** let one patterned sweater carry the outfit. Pair it with plain trousers, a solid overcoat and simple leather boots. A Fair Isle or Nordic sweater looks especially good with a full beard, because the texture matches the rugged, warm mood.

## 4. Chocolate Brown and Jewel Tones Replace Beige

After several seasons of "quiet luxury" oatmeal and grey, color is back in knitwear. Grazia USA reports that neutrals are sharing the spotlight with jewel tones and one star shade in particular: chocolate brown. The magazine says designers from Hermès to Tod's have treated it as the new black, and that a cocoa crewneck with dark denim "feels instantly richer." An emerald or ruby cardigan over black tailoring is its other suggestion.

GQ agrees that after years of neutrals, designers are making a compelling case for sweaters "in shades that are harder to ignore." For men, chocolate brown is the easiest way in. It works with navy, grey, cream, camel and denim, and it flatters most skin tones and beard colors, from blond and red to dark brown and salt-and-pepper.

**How to wear it:** a chocolate brown cashmere crewneck with indigo jeans and brown suede boots, or a brown V-neck over a cream shirt with charcoal trousers. Save brighter jewel tones for one piece per outfit.

## 5. Fewer Runway Sweaters, More Meaning in Each One

Here is a data point that explains the season. According to T-Fashion's analysis of the fall/winter 2026 menswear shows, the classic sweater knit appeared in **4.5% of looks**, down from 6.3% in FW25. In total, 121 designers showed the trend across 275 looks.

Fewer sweaters on the runway does not mean knitwear is fading. Read alongside Robb Report's observations, it suggests that designers are showing fewer filler knits and more pieces with something to say. For your own wardrobe, the lesson is the same: buy fewer sweaters, but make each one count in fiber, fit and design.

## 6. Cashmere as a Strategy, Not a Status Symbol

Grazia USA puts it neatly: cashmere in fall/winter 2026 "is less about status and more about strategy." A well-cut crewneck or turtleneck in black, grey or camel is the piece you repeat all season, with leather, with jeans and under tailoring.

How do you know you are buying good cashmere? Grazia's advice is practical: check the label for a higher cashmere content and feel for **bounce, not limpness**. A good knit springs back when you squeeze it. Construction matters too. Johnstons of Elgin, for example, describes its Reuben crew neck as 100% cashmere, knitted in its Hawick mill in Scotland from 2-ply yarns, a weight it says feels fluid and light enough for spring and autumn while still layering well in winter.

**What to look for in a luxury cashmere sweater:**

- **Fiber content:** 100% cashmere, or a clearly stated high percentage in a blend.
- **Ply:** 2-ply is a versatile, durable all-season weight; heavier plies suit deep winter.
- **Hand feel:** springy and dense, not thin, slippery or limp.
- **Finishing:** neat ribbed collars, cuffs and hems that hold their shape.
- **Origin and maker:** established mills in Scotland and Italy have long track records.

## 7. How to Wash, De-Pill and Store Cashmere

A fine sweater is only an investment if it lasts. Wirecutter's cashmere care guide recommends hand-washing in cool water with a gentle wool or cashmere wash, pressing the water out instead of wringing, rolling the sweater in a towel and drying it flat. Never tumble-dry cashmere and never hang it wet, because it will stretch.

Pilling is normal. It comes from friction, so it shows first under the arms and where a bag strap or seatbelt rubs. J.Crew's cashmere care guide suggests removing pills gently with a cashmere comb, and rotating your sweaters so each one gets time to rest.

For storage, fold sweaters instead of hanging them, keep them in a cool, dry and dark place, and wash them before you put them away at the end of the season. Cedar blocks and breathable cotton bags help.

## Beard and Knitwear: Small Details That Matter

A beard and a great sweater are a natural pair, but there are a few details worth knowing. High turtlenecks and chunky roll necks can rub a long beard and leave it frizzy, so open necklines such as V-necks and knit polos are kinder to longer beards. Beard oil and balm can transfer onto fine knits, so let them absorb for a few minutes before you get dressed. And if a coarse beard catches on delicate cashmere around the collar, a smoother, tighter knit holds up better than a loose, fuzzy one.

## FAQ: Men's Sweater Trends Fall 2026

### What sweaters are in style for men in fall 2026?

The key men's sweater trends for fall 2026 are the V-neck comeback, knit polos and collared cardigans, statement knits in Fair Isle, Nordic and windowpane patterns, and richer colors led by chocolate brown. A fine cashmere crewneck or turtleneck remains the essential base.

### Is the V-neck sweater back in style for men?

Yes. GQ lists the "'90s heartthrob V-neck" as a key knit for fall 2026. Wear it over a crewneck tee or a crisp button-up, with dark denim or flannel trousers.

### How can you tell if a cashmere sweater is good quality?

Check the label for a high cashmere content, and squeeze the knit: good cashmere feels dense and springs back, while weak cashmere feels thin or limp. Neat ribbed edges and a clear ply, such as 2-ply, are further signs of quality.

### How do you wash a cashmere sweater without ruining it?

Hand-wash it in cool water with a gentle wool or cashmere wash, press out the water without wringing, roll it in a towel and dry it flat. Do not tumble-dry it or hang it while wet.
