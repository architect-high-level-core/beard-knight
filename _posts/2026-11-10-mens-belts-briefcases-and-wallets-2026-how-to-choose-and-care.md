A belt, a briefcase and a wallet are the leather companions a man actually uses every working day. They are not decoration, and they are not the place to improvise. In 2026 the most elegant approach is still the quiet one: a belt that sits comfortably with your shoes, an office bag with enough structure for a city day, a slim wallet that does not swell a jacket pocket, and a short habit of care so the leather lasts. This guide explains how to match a belt to your shoes, how to choose a briefcase or a document bag, what a slim wallet should hold, and how to look after calfskin once you have it. The houses worth starting with in the United States are Hermès, Berluti, Bottega Veneta, Tom Ford, Valextra and Smythson.

> **Key takeaways**
> - A leather belt and leather shoes do not have to be a perfect match, but they should belong to the same family: black with black, browns that are closely related.
> - Do not wear a brown belt with black shoes. Shades of brown may differ; black does not have shades that will rescue a mismatch.
> - A slimmer belt and a quieter buckle read as dressier. With a well-cut suit, many stylists still prefer no belt at all.
> - For the office, a structured briefcase or a rigid document bag remains the most formal choice; a tote is more casual, a backpack more casual still.
> - A slim wallet in compact crossgrain or calfskin, with only the cards you use, is more elegant than a thick bifold stuffed with receipts.
> - Keep leather away from rain, heat and direct sun, wipe it with a soft dry cloth, and condition it sparingly.

## Should Your Belt Match Your Shoes?

The short answer, from Permanent Style's Simon Crompton, is no — not exactly. A leather belt and leather shoes do not have to match. They should, however, be closely related. A mid-brown belt with dark-brown shoes is fine. So is a brown leather belt with suede shoes, even when the shades are not identical. In one of Crompton's own examples, a dark-brown woven-leather belt sits perfectly well with mid-brown suede loafers. The two are in the same family, and that is enough.

Black is less forgiving. Because black does not really come in shades, a brown belt will almost always look odd against black shoes. The brown would have to be extremely dark for that not to be the case, and it is safer simply to wear a black belt with black shoes — or, given how formal black shoes are, to skip the belt entirely and wear trousers without loops. GQ's stylists come to a similar conclusion from the other direction. Michael Fisher puts it as convention: the belt should coordinate with your shoes, if not by color then by style, and you should not mix black and brown accessories. Avo Yermagyan adds that finishes should agree as well: matte with matte, patent with patent, and as little hardware as the outfit needs.

The material can flex more than the color. A leather belt may be worn with suede shoes if the colors stay in the same area. Once the belt is canvas or webbing rather than leather, the need to harmonize with leather shoes largely falls away.

Width and formality should follow the clothes. A slimmer strap and a smaller, quieter buckle look dressier; a wider, more rugged belt belongs with jeans or casual trousers. Hermès and Berluti typically cut dress straps at 32 to 38 mm, which is the right scale for tailored trousers. GQ's Jeanne Yang makes the same point in plainer language: the simpler the buckle, the slimmer the width, and the less rugged the leather, the dressier the belt will look. Tom Ford's T Icon belt, at 40 mm, sits at the broader end of that dress range and is reversible, which is useful if you own both black and brown shoes and prefer one belt to cover both.

There is a further option, and in tailored clothing it is often the best one. Several stylists quoted by GQ argue that a suit, if it is properly fitted, does not need a belt at all, and that a belt can add bulk at the waist. Crompton has long preferred trousers without loops for the smartest clothes, and uses a belt more often now only when he is wearing a jacket and trousers as separates and wants a little extra interest. If your tailor can cut side adjusters instead of loops, the problem disappears. If you do wear a belt with a suit, keep it close in color and entirely unshowy.

## The Office Bag: Briefcase, Attaché and What Comes After

The bag you carry to a meeting is judged faster than the belt, because it is larger and it arrives first. Permanent Style's advice is that color matters less here than it does with shoes, and that formality of shape matters more. An attaché or a rigid briefcase is the most formal; a soft briefcase or a structured document bag comes next; a tote is more casual; a backpack, however expensive, is more casual still. If you wear a suit with black shoes and a black belt most days, a black briefcase is the most elegant partner. If you want one bag that can also travel with more relaxed clothes, a dark brown is often the more useful investment.

Valextra's Premier Briefcase 24H is a clear statement of that first, formal choice. The Milan house cuts it in Pavé calfskin, with a rigid structure, protective metal feet, a cushioned top handle and an interior with two fan-shaped pockets. On the official U.S. site the black version is $4,300, and the bag's dimensions are 39 cm wide, 30 cm high and 6 cm deep. It is a document case rather than a weekend holdall, which is precisely the point. Valextra also makes the Iside and Avietta briefcases for men who want a slightly different silhouette, but the Premier remains the house's most uncompromising office shape.

You do not need to match the bag to the belt and the shoes with jeweller's precision. Crompton has long carried a matte-brown Hermès-style briefcase with smarter clothes, and readers of Permanent Style have pointed out, correctly, that men in the City have worn black shoes and a brown bag for decades without looking unfinished. The test is whether the bag is generally smart enough, not whether it is the same dye lot as the Oxfords. What you should avoid is a soft casual backpack over a well-cut jacket, which spoils the shoulder and announces the wrong kind of commute.

If you only carry a notebook, a slimmer document case will look more considered than a half-empty 24-hour bag. Hardware should be quiet, in one finish. A deep burgundy or very dark brown bag is often more versatile than a pale tan, which can look showy against a navy suit.

## The Slim Wallet, and What Belongs in It

A thick wallet ruins the line of a jacket as surely as a wide, ornamental belt. The modern luxury default is a slim bifold or a compact card wallet in a hard-wearing calfskin, sized for the cards and notes you actually use, not for a private archive of receipts, old tickets and loyalty cards you have not touched in a year.

Smythson's Panama leather is a good example of that thinking. The house's crossgrain calf, named after the Panama diary Frank Smythson designed in 1908, is lightweight, flexible and tough, and it has become one of the most recognizable leathers in British accessories. The 8 Card Slot Wallet in Panama, on the official U.S. site, is $335. It measures 11 by 10 cm, has a smooth calf interior, eight card pockets and two note pockets, and is made in Romania. It is small enough for a jacket or a trouser pocket, and large enough that you are not forced into a separate cardholder and a money clip. A 6 Card Slot Wallet in the same collection, at $300, is even slimmer if you carry less.

Tom Ford's grain-leather bifolds and Bottega Veneta's Intrecciato wallets offer other answers to the same problem, with texture or a clean architectural line instead of a logo. In every case the principle is the same: buy the smallest well-made wallet that holds what you need, then take out what you do not need.

A travel wallet is a different tool, not a substitute for the daily one. Keep the everyday wallet for the city.

## How to Care for Belts, Bags and Wallets

Luxury leather is not fragile, but it is particular. Valextra's care guide is a useful standard for structured calfskin, and Berluti's advice for Venezia is a useful standard for belts that are meant to patinate. Keep the piece away from rain, humidity, heat and long spells of direct sun. Avoid contact with alcohol, perfume, oils and cosmetics. Wipe leather and metal hardware with a clean, dry, soft, light-colored cloth. If water does reach the surface, do the same at once and let the piece dry slowly, away from a radiator or a heater vent.

Do not reach for a general-purpose waterproofing spray unless the maker recommends it; Valextra specifically advises against waterproofing products on its leather, and against loading a bag so heavily that the shape collapses. Store a briefcase upright on its feet, in its original bag or box, with tissue or the supplied cushion inside so the sides do not fall in. Roll a shoulder strap rather than folding it sharply. Keep dark leather away from light-colored cloth, and light leather away from dark cloth, to avoid color migration. Belts should be stored coiled loosely or hung, not crushed in a drawer under other accessories.

Conditioning should be rare and light. Berluti suggests a clear leather wax after a wipe-down, then a firm buff with a polishing glove. Once or twice a year is enough for a belt or a wallet that is not ridden hard in wet weather. A briefcase that only sees dry sidewalks may need even less. If a house offers its own repair and refinishing service — Berluti and Valextra both do — use it when the edges wear or a handle loosens, rather than trying to rebuild the piece at home.

## FAQ

### Does a men's belt have to match the shoes exactly?

No. Permanent Style's advice is that a belt and shoes should be closely related, not identical. Mid-brown and dark brown may be worn together, and a leather belt may be worn with suede shoes in a similar shade. Black shoes should be worn with a black belt, or with no belt at all.

### Should I wear a belt with a suit?

Only if the trousers have loops and you want a very quiet belt in the same family as the shoes. Many stylists, and many tailors, prefer trousers with side adjusters and no belt, because a well-cut suit does not need one and a belt can add bulk at the waist.

### What is the most formal office bag for a man?

A structured briefcase or a rigid document bag, in black or a dark brown, is still the most formal choice. A tote is more casual, and a backpack is more casual still, even when it is made from excellent leather.

### How slim should a men's wallet be?

Slim enough that it does not distort a jacket or trouser pocket. An 8-card or 6-card wallet in compact calfskin, emptied of receipts and unused cards, is the right scale for daily wear.

### How should I care for a leather belt, briefcase or wallet?

Keep it away from rain, heat and strong sun, wipe it with a soft dry cloth, and condition it only occasionally with a product the maker would accept. Store briefcases upright and belts loosely coiled or hung. Use the house's own repair service when edges or handles wear.
